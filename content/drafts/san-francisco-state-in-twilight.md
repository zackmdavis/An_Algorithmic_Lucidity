I've been crashing Prof. Lai's "Advanced Topics" class this semester, on Fourier analysis plus enough functional analysis to get by (since the University can't afford to offer functional analysis as a regular class anymore). It's not obviously a good use of my time, but now that I've started, I have to see it through. I could try to tell a story about _impact_: I am leveling up my math chops, which will be useful for understanding natural abstractions or singular learning theory or mechanistic explanation, which will be useful for—but maybe it's less embarrassing to not try to sell a story. Call it luxury consumption during garbage time. I've lived a good life, in some ways. I could choose to drown in oceans of regret over all the songs I never finished and stories I never wrote and math I never learned when I was young, when there was time for these things to matter—or I could choose to do something with the time I have now, which doesn't. Apparently, I am choosing to do some analysis.

Only it's not quite the same as it was in the before-time. On 7 September, I was struggling with the proof of Dirichlet's test.

Afterwards, I took the 28 bus back to Daly City BART station. At the station, the bus stopped before pulling all the way up to the curb. Apparently a car was in the way: the bus driver honked a few times, then more frequently, then in one long, continuous honk. Why wasn't the car moving? "It's a Waymo!" one of the passengers said. "Fucking Waymo," said someone else. "Bring back human drivers!" said a man. The bus driver gave up and opened the doors, still short of the curb. Walking to the station among the throng, I saw the offending Waymo had moved out of the way; it was unclear what had aroused it to motion.
