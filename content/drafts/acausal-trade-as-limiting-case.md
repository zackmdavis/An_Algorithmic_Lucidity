There's this idea of acausal trade, where I do something you want because I predict that you'll do something I want &c. A lot of people think this is spooky: what do you mean, you can trade without causality? You're open to trade offers from anyone in the universe (past or future), or even other universes?

It's not that spooky. We're familiar with the concept of making deals, by using back-and-forth communication to pin down the terms of the deal.

In acausal trade, you're _inferring_ the terms of the deal rather than communicating them using physical signals.

But even in ordinary deals, there's some amount of inference involved. Real-world contracts don't pin down every detail; there's still an element of inferring what the other guy wants (because you think that he'll be trying to infer what you want to uphold his end of the deal). (And no matter how explicit a contract is, it's still your brain that has to interpret the words and infer the meaning; the symbols themselves are inert.)

The amount that's spelled out in the contract vs. needing to be inferred is quantitative. Less extreme than the explicit contract, you can bring your items to the swap meet and say, "Trade?"

Or prisoners deciding when to rush the guards and start a riot can give each other a knowing look. They don't need to negotiate a long contract of "I'll rush him if you do". A sufficient model of each other's goals makes the knowing-looking enough communication to make the deal go through.

Acausal trade is just the limiting case where there's _no_ physical communication, and it's _all_ inference. But it's really not that much spookier than the prisoners (who only need a little bit of physical communication).
